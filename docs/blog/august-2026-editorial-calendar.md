## Topic Briefs

### 1. How to Set a Realistic Monthly Running Goal

- **Reader problem:** A runner wants a motivating monthly target without
  choosing an arbitrary or unsuitable distance.
- **Distinct angle:** Explain how recent activity, available days, event
  format, and gradual progression inform a personal target. Do not prescribe
  one distance for every runner.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/events`,
  `/blog/how-accumulated-distance-challenges-work`, and
  `/blog/how-to-choose-between-a-5k-10k-21k-or-distance-challenge`.
- **Source requirements:** WHO physical-activity guidance and current
  HelloRun accumulated-challenge behavior.

### 2. How to Write Clear Virtual Run Rules Participants Can Follow

- **Reader problem:** An organizer needs rules that runners can understand
  before registering or submitting evidence.
- **Distinct angle:** Cover dates, eligible activities, proof requirements,
  review outcomes, corrections, public results, rewards, and organizer
  contact responsibilities without repeating the full organizer playbook.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/how-it-works`, `/faq`,
  `/blog/what-counts-as-valid-run-proof`, and `/organiser-terms`.
- **Source requirements:** Current HelloRun event fields, review workflows,
  Organiser Terms, and Community Guidelines.

### 3. The Run-Walk Method: A Beginner-Friendly Way to Build Endurance

- **Reader problem:** A beginner cannot yet run continuously and may assume
  walking means failure.
- **Distinct angle:** Present planned running and walking intervals as a
  flexible pacing structure, with effort guided by the talk test rather than
  a universal interval prescription.
- **Recommended internal links:**
  `/blog/beginner-5k-training-plan-new-runners`,
  `/blog/beginners-guide-to-running-pace`, and `/events`.
- **Source requirements:** WHO gradual-activity guidance and the CDC talk
  test. Include a clear medical and individual-variation boundary.

### 4. A Participant Communication Timeline for Virtual Running Events

- **Reader problem:** An organizer is unsure what participants need to hear
  before, during, and after an event.
- **Distinct angle:** Build a practical timeline for registration
  confirmation, reminders, activity windows, proof deadlines, corrections,
  final review, results, and recognition.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/how-it-works`, `/contact`,
  `/blog/virtual-run-checklist-for-first-time-organizers`, and
  `/organiser-terms`.
- **Source requirements:** Current HelloRun notification, event-promotion,
  registration, submission, and final-review behavior.

### 5. How to Choose a Safe Route for Your Virtual Run

- **Reader problem:** A runner needs a route that fits the distance while
  accounting for traffic, lighting, weather, surface, and emergency access.
- **Distinct angle:** Use a pre-run route checklist and a stop-or-reschedule
  decision. Avoid presenting any route as guaranteed safe.
- **Recommended internal links:**
  `/blog/running-safety-tips-early-morning-night-runs`,
  `/blog/running-during-rainy-season-philippines`, and `/events`.
- **Source requirements:** Current PAGASA forecasts and advisories, local
  authority instructions, and established pedestrian-safety guidance.

### 6. How to Design Fair Distance Categories and Challenge Goals

- **Reader problem:** An organizer needs categories that are understandable,
  achievable for different participants, and compatible with event results.
- **Distinct angle:** Separate single-result distances from accumulated
  goals, explain category naming and pricing clarity, and prevent overlapping
  or misleading choices.
- **Recommended internal links:**
  `/blog/how-to-organize-a-virtual-run-a-practical-guide-for-event-organizers`,
  `/blog/how-accumulated-distance-challenges-work`, and `/how-it-works`.
- **Source requirements:** Current HelloRun category, distance-pricing,
  accumulated-goal, leaderboard, and certificate behavior.

### 7. Post-Run Recovery Basics: Rest, Hydration, and When to Ease Back

- **Reader problem:** A runner wants to know how to approach the period after
  an ordinary run without mistaking general education for medical diagnosis.
- **Distinct angle:** Cover routine rest, fluids, food, sleep, and gradual
  return while clearly separating ordinary recovery from symptoms requiring
  professional or emergency help.
- **Recommended internal links:**
  `/blog/how-to-run-safely-during-hot-and-humid-weather`,
  `/blog/beginner-5k-training-plan-new-runners`, and `/faq`.
- **Source requirements:** WHO or comparable public-health guidance and
  qualified sports-medicine guidance. Avoid diagnosing injuries or promising
  recovery timelines.

### 8. A Fair and Consistent Run-Proof Review Checklist for Organizers

- **Reader problem:** Reviewers need to apply the same evidence standard
  across participants and explain corrections or rejection clearly.
- **Distinct angle:** Turn HelloRun's review states into a repeatable
  checklist covering event match, date, distance, duration, duplicate
  signals, private evidence, reviewer judgment, and correction reasons.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/blog/what-counts-as-valid-run-proof`,
  `/blog/why-a-virtual-run-submission-may-be-rejected`,
  `/data-usage-policy`, and `/organiser-terms`.
- **Source requirements:** Current proof-integrity, OCR-assistance,
  submission-review, rejection-reason, privacy, and audit behavior.

### 9. How to Build a Weekly Running Schedule Around Work or School

- **Reader problem:** A runner needs a realistic routine that fits changing
  responsibilities instead of an inflexible daily plan.
- **Distinct angle:** Show how to identify available time, mix effort and
  easier days, preserve recovery, and revise the plan after missed sessions.
- **Recommended internal links:**
  `/blog/how-to-stay-consistent-during-a-month-long-virtual-run`,
  `/blog/beginners-guide-to-running-pace`, and `/events`.
- **Source requirements:** WHO physical-activity guidance, CDC intensity
  guidance, and explicit individual-variation limits.

### 10. Data Privacy Checklist for Running Event Organizers

- **Reader problem:** An organizer receives participant and proof data but
  needs practical boundaries for access, exports, communications, and
  deletion.
- **Distinct angle:** Follow the participant-data lifecycle from collection
  through restricted review, authorized export, incident reporting, and
  end-of-purpose handling.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/privacy`, `/data-usage-policy`,
  `/organiser-terms`, and `/contact?topic=privacy_data`.
- **Source requirements:** Philippine National Privacy Commission guidance
  and the current HelloRun Privacy, Data Usage, and Organiser Terms.

### 11. What to Bring on Race Day: A Checklist for On-Site and Hybrid Events

- **Reader problem:** A participant needs a concise preparation list for the
  venue portion of an on-site or hybrid event.
- **Distinct angle:** Separate universal preparation from event-specific
  instructions, covering confirmation details, clothing, hydration,
  weather, timing, venue rules, and emergency contacts without inventing
  organizer requirements.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/events`, `/how-it-works`,
  `/blog/how-to-prepare-for-your-first-virtual-run`, and `/faq`.
- **Source requirements:** The selected event's published rules, PAGASA
  guidance, local authority instructions, and conservative public-health
  guidance.

### 12. How to Make Running Event Instructions More Inclusive and Accessible

- **Reader problem:** Organizers may unintentionally make event instructions
  difficult to understand, navigate, or act on.
- **Distinct angle:** Focus on plain language, meaningful headings,
  text alternatives, non-color status cues, accessible contact paths,
  adaptable participation details, and explicit eligibility boundaries.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/community-guidelines`, `/how-it-works`,
  `/contact`, and
  `/blog/how-schools-and-organizations-can-use-virtual-runs`.
- **Source requirements:** W3C accessibility guidance, current HelloRun
  accessibility patterns, and the event's actual supported accommodations.

### 13. Returning to Running After a Break: A Gradual Restart Plan

- **Reader problem:** A runner wants to resume activity after time away but
  should not simply repeat a previous workload.
- **Distinct angle:** Use a flexible restart framework based on present
  capacity, easy effort, gradual increases, rest, and reassessment rather
  than fixed performance promises.
- **Recommended internal links:**
  `/blog/beginner-5k-training-plan-new-runners`,
  `/blog/beginners-guide-to-running-pace`, and `/events`.
- **Source requirements:** WHO gradual-activity guidance and qualified
  public-health or sports-medicine guidance. Include explicit boundaries for
  illness, injury, pregnancy, chronic conditions, and concerning symptoms.

### 14. How to Close a Virtual Run: Final Reviews, Results, and Recognition

- **Reader problem:** An organizer needs a reliable end-of-event sequence
  after activity submission closes.
- **Distinct angle:** Explain final review queues, corrections, official
  standings, accumulated totals, certificate finalization, communications,
  unresolved cases, and record retention.
- **Recommended internal links:** `/how-it-works`,
  `/blog/how-leaderboards-work-virtual-running-events`,
  `/blog/how-accumulated-distance-challenges-work`, and
  `/organiser-terms`.
- **Source requirements:** Current HelloRun final-review, ranking,
  certificate, notification, audit, and privacy behavior.

   the intent without changing its purpose.

   internal-link path.
5. Confirm the final article will contain at least 500 substantive words,
   original HelloRun-specific examples, one page-level H1, meaningful
   headings, at least two useful internal links, and a practical next step.
